# Crew: real astronauts

The six crew members in the Crew screen are real astronauts. Their game roles, perks and
numbers are game rules (see `AVAILABLE_CREW` in `src/contracts/gameContract.ts`); their
names and bios are real, and every fact in a bio comes from the sources below. The pixel
portraits are game art, not likenesses.

## The facts behind each bio

- **Neil Armstrong.** Flew seven X-15 rocket-plane flights (1960–62). Commanded Gemini 8 (March 1966),
  the first docking of two spacecraft in orbit. Commanded Apollo 11 and became the first person to set
  foot on the Moon on July 20, 1969. (NASA)
- **Eileen Collins.** "The first female pilot and the first female commander in shuttle history." Pilot on
  STS-63 (February 3–11, 1995) and STS-84 (1997); commander of STS-93 (July 1999) and STS-114 (2005).
  Four flights, over 872 hours in space. (NASA)
- **Kalpana Chawla.** Born in Karnal, India. PhD in aerospace engineering (University of Colorado, 1988).
  From 1988 at NASA Ames she worked on computational fluid dynamics: simulating the air flow around
  aircraft such as the Harrier. Mission specialist and prime robotic arm operator on STS-87 (Columbia,
  November 19 – December 5, 1997). She died with the Columbia crew on STS-107, February 1, 2003. (NASA)
  Space.com and others describe her as the first Indian-born woman in space; NASA's biography does not use
  that wording, so the game's bio says "born in Karnal, India" instead.
- **Bernard A. Harris Jr.** Doctor of medicine (Texas Tech, 1982), internal medicine residency at the Mayo
  Clinic, trained as a flight surgeon; NASA clinical scientist and flight surgeon. Payload Commander on
  STS-63 (February 2–11, 1995), during which he "became the first African-American to walk in space."
  Also flew STS-55 (1993). (NASA)
- **Chiaki Mukai.** M.D. and Ph.D. in medical science; trained in surgery at Keio University Hospital.
  Payload specialist on STS-65 (Columbia, July 1994, the IML-2 science mission: human physiology, space
  biology, radiation biology) and STS-95 (Discovery, 1998, with John Glenn), conducting life science and
  space medicine experiments. JAXA calls her the "first female Asian astronaut to fly in space," which also
  makes her the first Japanese woman in space. (JAXA)
- **Scott Kelly.** Returned to Earth in March 2016 after a 340-day mission on the International Space
  Station (520 days in space in total, then the most of any U.S. astronaut). His identical twin Mark took
  part in the Twins Study on Earth. In January 2016 he shared photos of zinnias blooming in the station's
  Veggie plant system, after nursing them through mold and overwatering. NASA called it the first flower
  grown in the Veggie facility; plants had flowered in space before, so the game does not say "first flower
  in space." (NASA)
